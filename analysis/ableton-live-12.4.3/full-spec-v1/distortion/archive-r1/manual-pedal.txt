pedal:B002 HTML:1468 Pedal is a guitar distortion effect. In combination with Live’s Tuner, Amp and Cabinet effects, Pedal is great for processing guitar sounds. Pedal can also be used in less conventional settings, such as a standalone effect on vocals, synths or drums.
pedal:B003 HTML:1468 The Gain control adjusts the amount of distortion applied to the dry signal. Note that 0% does not mean zero distortion. It is recommended to dial the Gain back to 0% and slowly increase it until you get the desired output level. When placed in front of Pedal in a device chain, Utility’s Gain parameter can be used to lower the signal even further.
pedal:B004 HTML:1468 The global Output knob adjusts the overall output gain of the device.
pedal:B005 HTML:1468 You can choose between three different Pedal Types, each inspired by distortion pedals with their own distinct sonic characteristics:
pedal:B006 HTML:1468 Overdrive: warm and smooth
pedal:B007 HTML:1468 Distortion: tight and aggressive
pedal:B008 HTML:1468 Fuzz: unstable, with a “broken amp” sound
pedal:B009 HTML:1468 Pedal has a three-band EQ that adjusts the timbre of the sound after the distortion is applied. The EQ is adaptive, which means that the amount of resonance (or “Q”) increases as the amount of EQ boost increases.
pedal:B010 HTML:1468 The Bass control is a peak EQ, with a center frequency of 100 Hz. This is useful for boosting the “punch” in bass or drum sounds, or attenuating low frequencies of guitars.
pedal:B011 HTML:1468 The Mid control is a three-way switchable boosting EQ. The Mid Frequency switch sets the center frequency and range of the Mid control. The center frequency is the middle of the frequency range that the Mid control operates upon. The frequency range around this center value is narrower in the lowest switch setting and wider in the higher setting. This is common in guitar pedals where it is normal to make tight cuts and boosts at low frequencies, and wider cuts and boosts at high frequencies.
pedal:B012 HTML:1468 The center frequencies for the switch settings are:
pedal:B013 HTML:1468 Lowest setting, positioned on the left side: 500 Hz
pedal:B014 HTML:1468 Middle setting, positioned in the center: 1 kHz
pedal:B015 HTML:1468 Highest setting, positioned on the right side: 2 kHz
pedal:B016 HTML:1468 The Treble control is a shelving EQ, with a cutoff frequency of 3.3 kHz. This is useful for removing harsh high frequencies (or boosting them, if that’s your cup of tea!).
pedal:B017 HTML:1468 Tip: For a more fine-grained EQ post-distortion, simply leave these controls in their neutral position and instead use another EQ, such as EQ Eight.
pedal:B018 HTML:1468 The Sub switch toggles a low shelf filter that boosts frequencies below 250 Hz. You can use this in conjunction with the Bass control by turning Sub on and setting the Bass to -100%, or turning Sub off and setting the Bass to 100%.
pedal:B019 HTML:1468 The Dry/Wet slider adjusts the balance between the processed and dry signals.
pedal:B020 HTML:1468 Aliasing can be reduced by enabling Hi-Quality mode, which can be accessed via the device title bar’s context menu. This improves the sound quality, particularly with high frequency signals, but there is a slight increase in CPU usage.

pedal:B022 HTML:1468 29.28.1.1 Positioning Pedal in the Device Chain
pedal:B023 HTML:1468 The incoming signal will have an impact on how the distortion will respond. For example, adding a Compressor before Pedal in the device chain will give a more balanced end result. On the other hand, adding an EQ or filter with high gain and resonance settings before Pedal can yield a screaming distortion effect.
pedal:B024 HTML:1468 29.28.1.2 Techno Kick
pedal:B025 HTML:1468 Choose a suitable kick with a long decay (e.g., Kit-Core 909, with decay turned up). Then, choose the Distort pedal, activate the Sub switch, and dial in the Gain to your taste.
pedal:B026 HTML:1468 For added “whack”, move the Mid Frequency switch to the right-most position and increase the Mid control. For more “thump”, you can increase the Bass control.
pedal:B027 HTML:1468 To reduce the “air”, decrease the Treble control.
pedal:B028 HTML:1468 29.28.1.3 Drum Group Fizzle
pedal:B029 HTML:1468 To add “fizzle” to a drum group, choose the Fuzz pedal, increase the Gain to 50% and make sure the Sub switch is disabled.
pedal:B030 HTML:1468 Reduce the Bass and Mid controls to -100%, and adjust the Mid Frequency Switch to taste. Increase the Treble to 100%.
pedal:B031 HTML:1468 Set the Output to -20dB. Then, turn the Dry/Wet slider down to 0% and slowly increase it until the drums are fizzling to your taste.
pedal:B032 HTML:1468 29.28.1.4 Broken Speaker
pedal:B033 HTML:1468 Select the Fuzz pedal, and make sure the Sub switch is disabled. Turn down the Bass control completely, and set the Treble to 25%.
pedal:B034 HTML:1468 Set the Mid control 100% and move the Mid Frequency switch to right-most position. Finally, set the Gain control to 100%.
pedal:B035 HTML:1468 29.28.1.5 Sub Warmer
pedal:B036 HTML:1468 To add upper harmonics and warmth to a simple sub bass, choose the OD pedal, turn on the Sub switch and turn up the Bass control. Then, slowly increase the Gain until the desired effect is reached. You can then cut or boost the mid frequencies using the Mid control.
