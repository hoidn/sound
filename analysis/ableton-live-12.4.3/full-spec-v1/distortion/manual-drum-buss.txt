drum-buss:B001 HTML:970 29.12 Drum Buss
drum-buss:B002 HTML:982 Drum Buss is an analog-style drum processor that was designed to add body and character to a group of drums, while gluing them together in a tight mix.
drum-buss:B003 HTML:982 The Trim slider lets you reduce the input level before any processing is applied to the signal.
drum-buss:B004 HTML:982 The Comp toggle applies a fixed compressor to the input signal before it is processed by the distortion. The compressor is optimized for balancing out groups of drums, with fast attack, medium release and moderate ratio settings, as well as ample makeup gain.
drum-buss:B005 HTML:982 There are three types of distortion which can be applied to the input signal. Each distortion type adds an increasing degree of distortion, while lending its own character to the overall sound:
drum-buss:B006 HTML:982 Soft: waveshaping distortion
drum-buss:B007 HTML:982 Medium: limiting distortion
drum-buss:B008 HTML:982 Hard: clipping distortion with bass boost
drum-buss:B009 HTML:982 For more intensity, it is possible to drive the input prior to distorting it. The Drive control lets you determine how much drive is applied to the input signal.
drum-buss:B010 HTML:982 Drum Buss combines commonly-used drum processing tools for shaping the mid-high range and filling out the low end, which we will look at in the following sections.
drum-buss:B011 HTML:982 29.12.0.1 Mid-High Frequency Shaping
drum-buss:B012 HTML:982 The mid-high frequency shaping tools are designed to add clarity and presence to drums such as snares and hi-hats.
drum-buss:B013 HTML:982 Crunch adjusts the amount of sine-shaped distortion applied to mid-high frequencies.
drum-buss:B014 HTML:982 The Damp control is a low-pass filter, which removes unwanted high frequencies that can occur after adding distortion.
drum-buss:B015 HTML:982 The Transients knob emphasizes or de-emphasizes the transients of frequencies above 100 Hz. Positive values add attack and increase sustain, resulting in a full, “punchy” sound. Negative values also add attack, but decrease sustain. This tightens up the drums, giving them a sharper, more crisp sound with less room and rattle.
drum-buss:B016 HTML:982 29.12.0.2 Low-End Enhancement
drum-buss:B017 HTML:982 Drum Buss’s low-end enhancement is made up of two tools: a resonant filter, which dramatically boosts bass frequencies, as well as a Decay control, which allows you to adjust the decay rate of both the incoming audio and the signal processed by the resonant filter. These tools help you to fill out the low-end of your drums.
drum-buss:B018 HTML:982 The Boom knob adjusts the amount of low-end enhancement that the resonant filter produces. The Bass Meter lets you see the Boom’s effect on the signal, which can be particularly useful if you can’t hear it.
drum-buss:B019 HTML:982 The Freq knob adjusts the frequency of the low-end enhancer. Force To Note lets you tune the low-end enhancer by setting its frequency to the value of the nearest MIDI note.
drum-buss:B020 HTML:982 The Decay control adjusts the decay rate of the low frequencies. When the Boom amount is set to 0%, the decay affects the incoming (post-drive and distortion) signal only. When the “Boom Level” is adjusted above 0%, the decay affects both the incoming and processed signals.
drum-buss:B021 HTML:982 To solo the result of the low-frequency enhancer, enable Boom Audition via the headphone icon.
drum-buss:B022 HTML:982 29.12.0.3 Output
drum-buss:B023 HTML:982 The Dry/Wet control adjusts the balance between the processed and dry signals.
drum-buss:B024 HTML:982 The Output Gain slider sets the amount of gain applied to the processed signal.
