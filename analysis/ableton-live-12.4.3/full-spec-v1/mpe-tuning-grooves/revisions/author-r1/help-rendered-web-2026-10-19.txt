L11: # MPE in Live FAQ
L12: 
L13: Learn how to use MPE (MIDI Polyphonic Expression) in Ableton Live to control pitch, slide, and pressure for individual notes with MPE-capable controllers. This FAQ covers MPE setup, compatible devices, and editing MPE modulation in Live.
L14: 
L15: cite4†Getting started with MPE L16: cite5†MPE compatibility L17: cite6†Editing and working with MPE L18: cite7†Troubleshooting MPE L19: 
L20: * * *
L21: 
L22: ## Getting started with MPE
L23: #### What is MPE?
L24: 
L25: MPE (MIDI Polyphonic Expression) allows you to control multiple instrument parameters simultaneously depending on how you press the notes on your MPE-capable MIDI controller.
L26: 
L27: With MPE you can change these individual values for every note in real time:
L28: 
L29:   * Pitch Bend (horizontal movement)
L30:   * Slide (vertical movement)
L31:   * Pressure
L32: 
L33: MPE MIDI messages are displayed once you record or draw a note, and you can edit them at any time.
L34: #### How can I use MPE?
L35: 
L36: You need an MPE-capable MIDI controller to use Live devices that have MPE functionality. Many third-party VST/AU plug-ins are also MPE-capable. To enable MPE:
L37: 
L38:   1. Connect your MPE-capable hardware, controller, or phone app with your computer
L39:   2. Open Live’s cite8†Preferences → Link/MIDI L40:   3. Activate the MPE button for each device
L41: 
L42: * * *
L43: 
L44: ## MPE compatibility
L45: #### Which Live devices are MPE-capable?
L46: 
L47: As of Live 12, all Live instruments support MPE.
L48: 
L49: In Live 11, the following Live devices support MPE:
L50: 
L51:   * Drift
L52:   * Wavetable
L53:   * Sampler
L54:   * Simpler (MPE settings can be changed by converting to Sampler)
L55:   * Arpeggiator
L56:   * AAS devices (Analog, Tension, Collision, Electric)
L57: 
L58: #### How do I know if my third-party plug-in is MPE-capable?
L59: 
L60: For third-party plug-ins, check the documentation or support page for your devices for whether they support MPE.
L61: #### Can I use MPE in my version and edition of Live?
L62: 
L63: MPE is available as of Live 11 and works with Live Intro, Standard, and Suite.
L64: 
L65: #### I don’t have an MPE controller. Can I still use MPE?
L66: 
L67: Yes, MPE editing can be used to write modulation for individual notes by using either Draw Mode or the mouse to edit a curve and its breakpoints.
L68: #### Can I use MPE with Push 3?
L69: 
L70: Push 3’s responsive pads offer the ability to interact dynamically with Live’s instruments and devices using MPE (MIDI Polyphonic Expression). Learn more about cite9†Getting Started with MPE on Push 3 .
L71: #### Can I use MPE with Push 1 and 2?
L72: 
L73: Push 1 and 2 support Poly Aftertouch in compatible Live devices and plug-ins. This allows you to apply different levels of Aftertouch to individual notes, making your music more expressive. To enable Poly Aftertouch with Push 2, press Setup on Push 2 and set Pressure to Poly.
L74: 
L75: * * *
L76: 
L77: ## Editing and working with MPE
L78: #### Can I use Pitch Bend for other parameters than pitch?
L79: 
L80: Yes, you can change the Modulation destination of the MPE instrument (depending on the instrument) or use Expression Control to map Pitch Bend to device parameters.
L81: #### How can I change MPE settings in Simpler?
L82: 
L83: While you can play Simpler using an MPE device, some MPE settings cannot be configured manually. To make changes, you can convert Simpler to Sampler, and convert it back to Simpler.
L84: 
L85: Note: Sampler cite10†is included†www.ableton.com in Live Suite or can be purchased separately for Live Intro or Standard.
L86: #### What's the difference between MPE and Polyphonic Aftertouch?
L87: 
L88: Polyphonic Aftertouch creates a MIDI message which is specific to each key. MPE creates three different types of MIDI messages (horizontal, vertical, pressure) which are specific to each key.
L89: #### How can I edit recorded MPE modulation?
L90: 
L91:   * Select the MIDI clip and click the Note Expression tab in the Clip View Editor.
L92:   * You can view each parameter by clicking the Show/Hide lane buttons.
L93:   * Similar to editing automation, you can move breakpoints, copy/paste/delete them, mark them, or use the draw mode.
L94:   * Morph between chords and add bends by connecting the curve of a note with any subsequent note.
L95: 
L96: cite11†Image: Bildschirmfoto_2021-02-16_um_12.04.57.png L97:   * MPE Control and Expression Control MIDI effects are designed for MPE devices, and are included as of Live 11’s Core Library.
L98:   * MPE Control influences the range and curve of incoming MPE data to improve expressive playing. It also lets you control non-MPE instruments with your MPE controller.
L99:   * Expression Control lets you add more MIDI sources with a transformation curve for each row and source, and can be used to integrate effects in an MPE chain.
L100: 
L101: * * *
L102: ## Troubleshooting MPE
L103: 
L104: #### Why does the Pitch Bend of my MPE controller move in incorrect increments?
L105: 
L106: The Pitch Bend range of your MPE controller and the MPE-capable instrument need to match for correct tracking.
L107: 
L108: #### Why can’t I control my effects polyphonically with Expression Control?
L109: 
L110: Controlling effects with Expression Control is only possible monophonically with last note priority.
L111: #### Why can’t I modulate Sample Selector and Sample Offset continuously?
L112: 
L113: This is expected behavior. Sample Selector and Sample Offset changes are only applied on Note On.
L114: 
L115: #### Why does playing my instrument with MPE result in silent notes?
L116: 
L117: You might experience that playing your instrument in MPE mode results in silent notes. The reasons and solutions for this problem affect certain hardware synths as well as multi-channel Plug-ins. L11: # Tuning Systems FAQ
L12: 
L13: Ableton Live's Tuning Systems allow you to work outside the 12-tone equal temperament system. Tunings work with most Live devices and MPE-capable plug-ins.
L14: 
L15:   * In Live, visit Help → Built-in Lessons → What’s New in Live → Tuning Systems.
L16:   * For tips on most features, type [?] to show Info View, then hover your cursor over the relevant feature. For in-depth explanations, visit the cite4†Live Manual.†www.ableton.com L17: 
L18:   cite5†iframe†www.youtube-nocookie.com L19: cite6†Finding and accessing Tuning Systems L20: cite7†Tunings with MIDI L21: cite8†Tunings with devices L22: cite9†About Tuning Systems L23: ### Finding and accessing Tuning Systems
L24: 
L25: How do I know if a Tuning System is active?
L26: When a Tuning System is activated, it will be displayed in the Tuning view, below Live’s Browser sidebar.
L27: cite10†Image L28: 
L29: How is the list of available tunings organized?
L30: Available tunings are sorted numerically and alphabetically.
L31: Why is my specific tuning system not available?
L32: The tuning systems included represent only a small subset of possible tunings. You can also import your system of choice, or a self-created system. Live will support any valid Scala file in the original format (.scl) or Ableton's format (.ascl).
L33: Can I import or create my own Tuning Systems files?
L34: Any existing Scala (.scl) file can be loaded into Live. Simply drag and drop the file into the Tuning section under Live’s Browser. You can also create and edit Scala files in our interactive Scala editor. Head to cite11†https://tuning.ableton.com/†tuning.ableton.com to learn more.
L35: Can Tuning Systems be hot-swapped?
L36: It's not possible to hot-swap tuning systems, but they are quick to load. Just double-click a Tuning System in the Browser, or select it and press Enter.
L37: ### Tunings with MIDI
L38: 
L39: What happens when I export a MIDI Clip?
L40: What you see in the piano roll will be exported as part of the MIDI Clip, but information about what tuning it was created with will not be included. For example, an instance of note number 45 will be exported as note number 45, regardless of what pitch this may have yielded with a tuning applied.
L41: How do I preserve the sound of my MIDI Clip?
L42: The same MIDI Clip, created in one tuning, may sound very different if loaded with a different tuning applied, or if loaded in software that doesn’t support tunings. To export a MIDI Clip for use in a context that does not support tunings and have it sound as close as possible to the original, in Live’s Options menu enable “Retune Set on Loading Tuning Systems,” select the tuning  “12-TET(EDO),” then export the Clip. Then use Undo to return to your desired tuning.
L43: Are microtonal sharps and flats exported with the MIDI file for use in notation programs?
L44: MPE information for microtonal retuning is not included in MIDI file export.
L45: What happens to MIDI notes when I load or remove a tuning system? What does the option “Retune Set on loading Tuning Systems” do?
L46: When the Tuning System of a Live Set is deleted or changed, existing MIDI notes won't produce the same pitch. Live has two options to deal with this situation. When the Option menu setting "Retune Set on loading Tuning Systems" is enabled, MIDI notes in existing clips will be moved to the tuned note that is nearest in pitch, or deleted. As this is a destructive action, a pop-up will warn you about it.
L47: cite12†Image L48: 
L49: With "Retune Set on loading Tuning Systems" disabled, MIDI notes will retain the same note index in the new tuning as in the previous one. This change is non-destructive, so there is no pop-up. However, the notes likely won't play the same pitches as before.
L50: What does Bypass Tuning do?
L51: When the Bypass Tuning toggle in the track In/Out section is enabled, MIDI instruments and plug-ins receive the original MIDI without any retuning. This is important for instruments like Drum Rack, where the MIDI note number is typically used to select which drum to play, as opposed to instruments where the MIDI note is traditionally used to decide which pitch to play.
L52: Can I adjust how my MIDI controller maps to notes in an activated Tuning System?
L53: With a Tuning System loaded, MIDI tracks will have a drop down in the track In/Out section showing "All keys". Select a layout to determine how MIDI controllers map to the loaded tuning system for this track or create a Custom Controller Layout.
L54: ### Tunings with devices
L55: 
L56: What Live Instruments do Tuning Systems work with?
L57: All software Instruments native to Live, except Drum Synths, work with tunings.
L58: 
L59: Do tuning systems apply to external hardware?
L60: Tuning systems can be applied to external hardware if Live is configured to output MPE and the instrument supports it. Make sure MPE is enabled on the device and the per-note pitch bend range is set to ±48.  Learn more about cite13†Live's MIDI Preferences .
L61: Does the Tuner device follow Tuning Systems?
L62: As of Live 12.1, Tuner supports tuning systems. Tuner's reference pitch is determined by the selected tuning.
L63: 
L64: Can Tuning Systems be used with third-party plug-ins or Max for Live devices?
L65: Tuning systems apply to VST2, VST3 and AU devices if they are MPE-compatible, as well as to MPE-compatible Max for Live devices. Make sure MPE is enabled on the relevant device, and that the device’s per-note pitch bend range is set to ±48.
L66: ### About Tuning Systems
L67: 
L68: What is the purpose of Tuning Systems? Do I have to use them?
L69: You are welcome to use Live without exploring tunings. We encourage you to follow your own preferred tuning system, and to try different ones. Tuning systems is an optional feature for expanding available pitch possibilities, to include a range of conventions and approaches.
L70: Can the reference pitch be changed when using standard equal temperament tunings?
L71: The reference pitch can only be changed once a Tuning System is loaded. The Core Library includes the tuning 12-TET(EDO), which matches Live's standard equal temperament tunings, however it does not work with the existing scales functions.
L72: Is the loaded Tuning System saved with the Live Set?
L73: When saving a Live Set, the loaded Tuning System will be saved within the Live Set file (.als file). To export a loaded tuning to your User Library as an .ascl file, click the Save button in the Tuning section.
L74: cite14†Image L75: Why doesn't my .scl file produce the tuning I expect in Live?
L76: A .scl file defines a tuning system as a set of intervals, rather than determining the absolute frequencies of notes in the system. This means it defines the distance of each note in the system from some base pitch, which must be set via a “reference pitch” before note frequencies can be calculated. For example, in 12TET (twelve tone equal temperament tuning), the reference pitch A3 (the note A in the third octave) is typically defined with a frequency of 440Hz.
L77: Applying a different value, such as 432 Hz, results in different frequencies being calculated for all notes, even though the tuning system is still 12TET.
L78: A basic .scl file contains no reference pitch. In some cases, this can be supplied with a paired .kbm file. However, many software tools that import .scl files don’t support .kbm files. In this case, the software using the .scl file must apply a reference pitch, which can either be configurable via the tool’s UI or hardcoded. Live does not support .kbm files, but the .ascl files shipped with Live, unlike basic .scl files, do include a reference pitch.
L79: When a .scl file without a reference pitch is loaded, Live generates a reference pitch, in order to determine frequencies for all the notes in the tuning system, by assigning the frequency 440 Hz to a note near the middle of the 0-127 MIDI range. The remaining notes in the system are then derived from this reference. If you have a .scl file you’ve used previously in some other tool, it may sound different in Live, since that tool may have used a different strategy to determine the reference pitch.
L80: To correct this, you can try to determine the reference pitch used by that tool and adjust the reference pitch used in Live via the tuning UI. You can also do this “by ear”, and simply tweak the reference pitch in Live until it matches the tuning in the other tool.
L81: Once you’ve corrected the reference pitch, you can export your tuning from Live as a .ascl file so that you don’t need to repeat these steps every time you use that tuning in Live – the tuning will be exported as a .ascl file containing the custom reference pitch you set. Just click the Save icon in the tuning UI.
L82: Where can I learn more about Live’s Tuning Systems?
L83: Click the right-most button in Live’s Tuning section to access cite15†Ableton’s Tuning website†tuning.ableton.com . If more information about the loaded Tuning System is available,  it will directly be displayed there.

L11: # ASCL Specification
L12: Learn how to use the ASCL file format, an Ableton-specific extension to the SCL (Scala) format used to store tuning system information. The example below specifies supported directives for reference pitch, note names, note range, and documentation as of Ableton Live 12.1.
L13: 
L14:     `! ASCL Specification
L15:     !
L16:     ! ASCL is an Ableton-specific extension to the SCL file format originally developed for
L17:     ! use with the Scala software to store tuning system information. It is a backwards
L18:     ! -compatible format where all Ableton-specific extensions are stored in SCL comments
L19:     ! (whose lines start with !). That means that any ASCL file can be read by software
L20:     ! capable of reading SCL files.
L21:     !
L22:     ! This specification is itself an ASCL file.
L23:     !
L24:     ! To start with, the file contains a text description, an integer number of notes per
L25:     ! octave and a list of pitches for each of these notes in either cents (expects a decimal
L26:     ! number) or integer ratio (e.g. 5/4). These notes are defined in relation to an implied
L27:     ! but not written zeroeth index. The last number gives the size of the psuedo-octave.
L28:     ! This format is described in more detail by the official SCL file documentation:
L29:     !
L30:     !   https://www.huygens-fokker.org/scala/scl_format.html
L31:     !
L32:     !
L33:     An example tuning system to document the ASCL format. If loaded in Live, the short description on this line will be shown in the info text associated with this tuning.
L34:      12
L35:     !
L36:      100.0
L37:      200.0
L38:      300.0
L39:      400.0
L40:      500.0
L41:      600.0
L42:      700.0
L43:      800.0
L44:      900.0
L45:      1000.0
L46:      1100.0
L47:      1200.0
L48:     !
L49:     !
L50:     ! # General notes for Ableton extensions
L51:     !
L52:     !   All extended data is stored in comment lines with the prefix @ABL which come after
L53:     !   the list of note pitches. All arguments to these definitions are separated by spaces.
L54:     !   If you wish your argument to include a space in it, surround it with double-quotes:
L55:     !   `"`
L56:     !
L57:     !   All Scala files that implement Ableton extensions must be in UTF-8 format. A
L58:     !   compliant parser for ABL extensions must reject any file that contains Ableton
L59:     !   extensions but is not in UTF-8 format.
L60:     !
L61:     !   Note, if you wish to temporarily disable a directive, simply add an extra ! sign at
L62:     !   the beginning of the line, and it will be ignored.
L63:     !
L64:     !
L65:     ! # Supported directives:
L66:     !
L67:     ! -- REFERENCE_PITCH - set a reference pitch for this tuning
L68:     !     Arguments:
L69:     !       Octave number (integer from -2 to 8)
L70:     !       Note index within the octave- which note pitch is the frequency set for
L71:     !         (positive integer, 0-indexed, less than the number of note pitches in this
L72:     !         tuning system. The zeroeth pitch is the implied 0-cent first note pitch.)
L73:     !       Frequency (nonzero positive float, in Hz)
L74:     !
L75:     ! @ABL REFERENCE_PITCH 4 9 440.0
L76:     !
L77:     !
L78:     ! -- NOTE_NAMES - set default names for the pitch classes in this tuning
L79:     !     Arguments:
L80:     !      A sequence of note names for every note pitch in the tuning system. The first
L81:     !        entry in the sequence applies to the zeroeth note.
L82:     !
L83:     ! @ABL NOTE_NAMES C C♯/D♭ D D♯/E♭ E F F♯/G♭ G G♯/A♭ A A♯/B♭ B
L84:     !
L85:     !
L86:     ! ## Note range directives:
L87:     !
L88:     !   These directives set the range of notes considered valid in the tuning system. You
L89:     !   can use one of either of these directives. It is optional to include a NOTE_RANGE
L90:     !   directive. It is an error to provide more than one NOTE_RANGE_BY_XXX directive in
L91:     !   a file.
L92:     !
L93:     ! -- NOTE_RANGE_BY_FREQUENCY
L94:     !     Arguments:
L95:     !       Minimum frequency in Hz (float from 4.0 to 21000.0)
L96:     !       Maximum frequency in Hz (float from 4.0 to 21000.0, optional)
L97:     !
L98:     ! -- NOTE_RANGE_BY_INDEX
L99:     !     Arguments:
L100:     !      Minimum octave (-99 to 99)
L101:     !      Minimum note pitch index within the octave (integer)
L102:     !      Maximum octave (-99 to 99, optional)
L103:     !      Maximum note pitch index within the octave (integer, optional)
L104:     !
L105:     ! Without a NOTE_RANGE directive, the notes available in the tuning system will be
L106:     ! centered around A3 440.0 Hz (MIDI note number 69) unless the tuning system has 12 notes
L107:     ! per pseudo-octave. All other notes in the tuning system will be calculated accordingly
L108:     ! on either side: 64 notes below the center point, and 63 notes above it.
L109:     !
L110:     ! If there are 12 notes per pseudo-octave it sets the note closest to A3 440.0 Hz
L111:     ! to note index 69 in the tuning. The 12 notes per pseudo-octave special case is
L112:     ! implemented in order for the tuning system note indices to match the default MIDI note
L113:     ! numbers and thus remain MIDI keyboard compatible by default for 12-note keyboard-based
L114:     ! tuning systems.
L115:     !
L116:     ! Including a highest note or frequency in a NOTE_RANGE directive is optional
L117:     !
L118:     ! If a NOTE_RANGE directive is used, the first usable note in the tuning system will be
L119:     ! set to the one specified by the minimum note and octave index (NOTE_RANGE_BY_INDEX), or
L120:     ! to the first note at or above the minimum frequency (NOTE_RANGE_BY_FREQUENCY).
L121:     !
L122:     ! The maximum playable pitch will be the first note either:
L123:     ! - 127 steps above the minimum note
L124:     ! - closest to and below 21kHz
L125:     ! - the one set as a maximum (NOTE_RANGE_BY_INDEX)
L126:     ! - or the last note below or equal to the maximum frequency (NOTE_RANGE_BY_FREQUENCY)
L127:     !
L128:     ! If using NOTE_RANGE_BY_INDEX with a tuning where notes are not always in order of
L129:     ! increasing frequency (non-monotonically increasing), the minimum and/or maximum is
