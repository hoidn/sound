# Analog audio by extracted execution (T1.2)

**Question.** What audio does the bundled Analog instrument produce for note events and stored
parameter values, observed by running its own x86_64 code? How do its oscillators, noise, two
filters, amplifiers, envelopes and LFOs act; how many voices does Polyphony give and how are they
allocated; what do glide and velocity do; and which comparison metric can a device spec state?

**Answer (X, draft).**

- **Rendering.** The [T1.2 harness](device-dsp-extracted.md#harness) renders Analog with no new
  bindings. Renders are bit-for-bit repeatable across re-runs, flush-to-zero, the clock seed and
  host blocks of 64, 256, 512 and 2,048 frames. Blocks of 333 frames move only events whose grid
  point lies in an earlier block, as the timing rule predicts. A warm-up of 11,264 silent frames
  leaves every later frame bit-identical.
- **Closed forms.** Most behavior reduces to exact laws that reproduce the renders to binary32
  rounding:
  - the oscillator frequency, including a 2ˣ approximation and a sample-rate term, within
    5.4 × 10⁻⁶ above 100 Hz;
  - the filter transfer functions of the 2-pole and 4-pole low-pass, band-pass, notch (2-pole)
    and high-pass types, as prewarped bilinear sections, residual 1.8 × 10⁻⁷;
  - the envelopes as a binary32 segment recursion, within 2.4 × 10⁻⁷ for 46 cases, including the
    quirks of long sustain times;
  - the level, balance, pan, volume and velocity laws, the drive and output saturators, and the
    modulation depths of the filter envelope, keyboard and LFO.
- **Voices.** Polyphony 0 to 9 gives 1, 2, 4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24, 28 or 32 voices. The oldest voice
  is stolen. Mono priority is High, Low or Last.
- **Randomness.** The noise source, the oscillator Noise shape, KeyboardError and VibratoError
  depend on the clock-seeded generator (X). The LFO random shapes do not.
- **Not settled.** Glide, unison, the envelope loop, legato and free-run modes, LFO sync and the
  LFO delay and fade, the closed forms of the formant and 4-pole notch filters, the random LFO
  shapes, and the smoothing of parameter changes. Live's protocol unknowns are those of
  [Q-025](../questions.md#q-025-device-host-protocol-and-device-audio-acceptance).

Evidence: [t12-analog-v1](../../analysis/ableton-live-12.4.3/r2/t12-analog-v1/manifest.json),
regenerated by `sh run.sh` (about one minute on eight workers). It holds:

- `harness/`, a byte-identical copy of the T1.2 loader;
- `an.py` (stored values → host values, rendering, estimators), `model.py` (the laws below as
  code, for checking only), `measure.py` (one area per topic), `cases.py` (reference renders) and
  `static_map.py` (static index map);
- `results/*.json`: one per area, `cases.json`, `static_map.json` and `fma_scan.txt`;
- `raw/DAN-*.wav` and `raw/DAN-*.script`: 47 reference renders (IEEE-float WAV, mono where both
  channels are bit-identical) and their exact call scripts.

The behavioral contract built on this page is [SPEC-DEVICE-ANALOG](../specs/device-analog.md).

## Slice and harness

Observed (`manifest.json` `external_inputs`). `Analog.bundle/Contents/MacOS/Analog`, SHA-256
`dddb93e754b87cf21e947a37ff2330f3754d2db07c26bc15785440782f8c3d4b`; x86_64 slice at offset 16,384,
500,880 bytes, SHA-256 `75c8d1cd8e10c7bcc2570c02c204d0860d725467353729eb1f2cef8594f2d113`. The slice
is referenced, not copied. The loader is `harness/` (identical SHA-256 to `t12-device-dsp-v1/harness`),

Static sources (S), read-only: the arm64 helper corpus of the same bundle
(`/var/tmp/sound-analysis/helpers/Analog/corpus`, slice SHA-256 `211bd67c…2f09ab`). The engine
also embeds a text patch at x86_64 slice offset 425,668 (`main_synth pm { … }`, 6,579 bytes). It
holds the built-in values that a render with no host values uses.

## Render protocol

Each render runs in a new process ([`an.py`](../../analysis/ableton-live-12.4.3/r2/t12-analog-v1/an.py)):

1. `CreateInstanceU`, then `SetSampleRateU` (44,100 or 48,000 Hz);
2. all 203 host values in index order;
3. the note events, as 16-byte records at absolute frames;
4. input flag 0, silent input, transport {not playing, 0, 120 BPM};
5. `CalcA` in host blocks of 512 frames.

Host values come from stored values by [N6](device-tables-native.md#analog-ultraanalog-203-entries-31-second-outputs)'s
conversions (`an.host`). The parameter sets are:

- **table**: the "Default when absent" column of SPEC-BUNDLED-DEVICE-PARAMETERS;
- **preset**: the values of the shipped Defaults preset, taken from N6's `spec_defaults.tsv` as
  parameter references only;
- **probe**: table, with both chain-1 envelopes at sustain 1 and the sine shape, so that a held key
  sounds steadily.

The first note is at frame 4,400, a control-grid point 100 periods after creation.

## Methods

- **Pitch.** An FFT peak, moved to the lowest sub-multiple with energy, refined by demodulation
  (`an.fund`). Modulated pitch is read from interpolated zero crossings of a sine (`an.zc_freq`).
- **Waveforms.** Least-squares harmonic amplitudes at the model frequency (`an.harmonics`).
- **Filters.** The noise source alone, with a fixed seed, is rendered with the filter off and on.
  The same seed gives the same noise sequence. A least-squares IIR fit of the filtered render on the
  unfiltered one (`measure.arx`) then gives the filter coefficients. A relative residual near
  10⁻⁷ means the filter is exactly that order and linear.
- **Envelopes.** The amplitude envelope's unstored ToggleDummy (host index 99) set to 0 removes
  the envelope. Live always sends 1, so this is only a measurement device. The render divided by
  that reference, with its key held, is the per-frame envelope gain (`measure.env_curve`). A 50 %
  pulse keeps the reference above 0.12 except at edges.
- **Laws.** Each law is written in `model.py` and compared with the renders.

## Static map of the parameter path

`static_map.py` → `results/static_map.json`. The wrapper's builder registers every host index:

| Kind | Indices | What the wrapper does with the value |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | 104 | clamps to 0..1, then sends the controller value c = 128v (v ≤ 0.5) or 126v + 1 |
| 2 | 1, 9, 10, 194, 196, 198, 200, 202 | clamps to the float range and sends it |
| 3 | 85 | clamps to 0..max and sends the integer |
| virtual | 0, 7, 195, 197, 199, 201 | Polyphony and the MPE targets, handled by the wrapper |

Each registration names an engine module and parameter. Module names are inferred from the
patch's module order and from the parameters each module receives. The module constructors declare
each parameter's curve and range. These curves are confirmed by renders below:

- exponential lo·(hi/lo)^(c/127), and a zero-capable variant that gives 0 at c = 0;
- linear, with n = 0.5 exactly at c = 64 and c/127 otherwise;
- square, n² with n = 0.5 at c = 64;
- integer.

Examples: envelope attack, decay and release 0.005 to 15 s; sustain time 0.1 to 1,000 s; cutoff
30 to 22,000 Hz; Q 0.1 to 120; oscillator and noise level 0.01 to 3; amplifier level 0.01 to 2;
LFO rate 3 to 1,000 per minute; vibrato rate 0.25 to 10 Hz.

## Results by area

### Timing and protocol (`timing.json`, `protocol.json`)

- The [T1.2 grid rule](device-dsp-extracted.md#analog-results) holds for 220 further events
  (note-ons at 44.1 and 48 kHz in blocks of 512, 64 and 333; note-offs at both rates). An event
  at frame f acts at grid point g′ (P = ⌊fs/1000⌋), and its first changed frame is g′ + 1.
- **Event bytes.** The channel nibble has no effect. A note-on with velocity 0 is a note-off.
  Release velocity has no effect. A note-off for a key that is not sounding changes nothing.
- **Silence and flag.** Output before the first note is exactly 0. The activity flag is 1 for the
  first two blocks after creation, then 0 until a note, then 1 while sounding.
- **Parameter changes.** A value set between blocks while a note sounds first changes the output
  at these offsets from the block start (13,312; g′ = 13,332):

  | Change | First changed frame |
  | --- | --- |
  | Volume | 13,332 |
  | OscillatorSemi, OscillatorDetune | 13,340 |
  | FilterCutoffFrequency | 13,376 |
  | OscillatorLevel, AmplifierLevel, AmplifierPan | 13,390 |
  | Amplifier AttackTime, while sustaining | 17,734 |

  The ramps after these points were not characterized.

### Pitch (`pitch.json`, 335 cases)

- The frequency of a held key is fs·A(p)/A(q), where A is the 2ˣ approximation that the
  [Corpus page](device-dsp-extracted.md) found. Here:
  - p = ((key − 69)·s + 9 + Transpose + 12·Octave)/12 + fine;
  - q = log₂(fs) − 8.03136 − OscOct − OscSemi/12 − detune;
  - the stretch s = lin(0.992..1.008) acts on the key's distance from A4 only;
  - fine = lin(±1/24 octave);
  - detune = ±((c − 64)/64)²/4 octave, sign that of c − 64;
  - log₂(fs) comes from binary32 `logf(fs)` × 1/ln 2.

  The arm64 VCO code (S, `0x35f80`, `0x37830`) has this structure. The constant 8.03136 is log₂ 261.6256.
- The render-to-model difference is at most 0.0093 cents (5.4 × 10⁻⁶) above 100 Hz. It is
  0.044 cents at 16 Hz, which is the estimator's limit there.
- **Consequences.**
  - C4 is 261.5371 Hz at 44.1 kHz (−0.585 cent from 12-TET) and 261.4782 Hz at 48 kHz (−0.976 cent).
  - Pitch classes deviate from 12-TET by the A(x) error profile, up to +1.34 cents at A.
  - The offset depends on frac(log₂ fs − 8.03136): equal for 22.05, 44.1 and 88.2 kHz, and for
    48, 96 and 192 kHz.
- KeyboardError gives a random offset per note: 11.3 cents at Error 1 for the seed tested. It is
  seed-dependent, and its distribution was not measured.

### Oscillators (`osc.json`, `subsync.json`)

Internal levels are given at oscillator gain 1 and balance gain 1. The output adds the amplifier,
pan and volume gains (below). The probe sine peaks at 0.36396 at the output.

| Shape | Waveform |
| --- | --- |
| 0 Sine | Amplitude 0.6987 (peak 0.7002). Harmonics 2 to 6 at −37.9, −52.5, −54.4, −58.5 and −62.4 dB |
| 1 Saw | Harmonic k is (1/πk)·(1 + e_k): a ±0.5 sawtooth, band-limited. e_k grows to +5.4 % at k = 30 for C4. DC −8 × 10⁻⁵ |
| 2 Rectangle | Harmonic k is (2/πk)·\|sin(πkd)\|·(1 + e_k), a pulse of height 1. Duty d = (c + 10)/274, from 3.65 % to 50 %. DC = 0.8872d − 0.1818 (residual 3 × 10⁻⁵) |
| 3 Noise | Seed-dependent noise |

- PulseWidth has no effect on Sine or Saw.
- The start phase is fixed at note-on: renders do not depend on the seed.
- **Sub** (OscillatorMode 0): adds a component one octave down at SubAmount gain exp0(0.01..1):
  - for Saw and Rectangle it is a square with the main waveform's amplitude (h1 0.3308 at amount 1
    for the probe);
  - for Sine it is a sine.
- **Sync** (mode 1): Saw and Rectangle are hard-synced at a ratio exp(1..20) of the key
  frequency. The strongest partial lies near that ratio. Sine is unaffected.
- **Pitch envelope** (`peg.json`): starts at ±exp(0.01..6) octaves, the sign that of the stored
  amount, and falls linearly to 0 over exp(0.01..10) s. The checks at fractions 0.05 to 0.95 of the
  time agree with linear within the estimator's 1–2 ms lag.

### Noise (`noise.json`)

- The raw source (Color 1) has a flat spectrum, kurtosis 1.80 (uniform) and RMS 0.375 at the
  output (probe, balance gain 1).
- Color is a one-pole low-pass y ← y + a(x − y):
  - a = min(1, 2πf/fs);
  - f = exp(c; 20, 22,000) Hz;
  - the cross-spectral transfer function matches within 0.16 dB and 1° (Color 0.1), and within
    0.02 dB from Color 0.4;
  - Color ≥ 0.9 is unfiltered.
- NoiseLevel follows the oscillator level law, and NoiseBalance the balance law.

### Levels, balance, pan and volume (`levels.json`, model in `model.py`)

- OscillatorLevel and NoiseLevel give gain exp(c; 0.01, 3); the default 0.8062 gives 1.0019.
- AmplifierLevel gives gain exp(c; 0.01, 2); the default 0.869 gives 1.0045.
- Volume gives gain 2(c/127)², with 0.5 at c = 64; the default gives 1.000.
- The models agree within 10⁻⁴ dB.
- **Balance.** The gain into filter 1 is S((1 − n)/4) and into filter 2 S(n/4), with n the host
  value's linear n. S is the polynomial sine
  6.094263φ + 6.094263φ² − 85.31969φ³ + 121.88526φ⁴ − 48.754105φ⁵. Host 0 gives exactly 1 and 0.
  The centre gives 0.718635, not 1/√2. Host = 1 − stored value: a stored Balance 1 sends everything
  to filter 1.
- **Pan.** Left S((1 − n)/4), right S(n/4), with no exact ends: S(1/4) = 0.99984.

### Filters (`filter.json`, `mod.json`, `routing.json`, `drive.json`)

Every result below is linear: the input level does not change it, below the saturators.

- **Cutoff.** f_c = 30·(22,000/30)^(c/127) Hz, with no midpoint case. It is independent of fs
  within 7 × 10⁻⁶. It is clamped to at most 0.49·fs and, after modulation, to at least 20 Hz.
- **Q.** Q = exp(c; 0.1, 120), limited to 100 for the 2-pole types.
- **2-pole types** (stored types 0, 2, 4, 6). The transfer function is the prewarped bilinear
  transform of the analog prototype, with g = tan(πf_c/fs), s = (1 − z⁻¹)/(g(1 + z⁻¹)) and
  D = s² + s/Q + 1:

  | Type | Name | Transfer function |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | 0 | LP12 | 1/D |
  | 2 | BP6 | s/D (peak gain Q) |
  | 4 | Notch2 | (s² + 1)/D |
  | 6 | HP12 | s²/D |

  The fitted coefficients agree with this to a residual of 1.8 × 10⁻⁷ (72 fits).
- **4-pole types.**
  - LP24 and HP24: two identical sections at Q_s = min(√Q, 100). LP24 agrees within 0.004 dB. HP24
    agrees in its passband; the fit is ill-conditioned in its stopband.
  - BP12: two BP6 sections at the full Q, within 0.12 dB.
  - Notch4 at Q above about 1 does not match two notch sections. Its closed form is open.
- **Formant types** (8, 9). Not identified; render-graded only.
- **Keyboard tracking.**
  - Cutoff: the cutoff moves by 4n² octaves per octave of key distance from 60 (0.25 → 0.254,
    0.5 → 1, 0.75 → 2.26).
  - Q: 4n² octaves of Q per octave.
  - A negative amount inverts.
- **Filter envelope.**
  - Cutoff: at full envelope the cutoff moves 16n⁴ octaves (0.25 → 0.064, 0.5 → 1, 0.75 → 5.11).
  - Q: 4n² octaves of Q.
  - The shift scales with the envelope level (sustain 0.5 halves it).
- **LFO.** On cutoff, 4n² octaves at full LFO.
- **Routing.**
  - Chain 2 renders bit-identically to chain 1 for the same settings.
  - FilterToFilter2 adds filter 1's output, at gain 2(c/127)² (no midpoint case), to filter 2's
    input. Amplifier 1 still receives filter 1's output.
  - With FilterControllee on, filter 2's cutoff is filter 1's cutoff × A((c − 64)/32), where c is
    filter 2's own cutoff controller. At c = 127 the factor is exactly 4.
  - Following a filter 1 that is off gives 20 Hz.
- **Drive** (0..6). Applied to the filter input:
  - input gain 1, √2 or 2 for Drive 1/4, 2/5 and 3/6;
  - then a saturator that is linear for |u| ≤ 1 and above that gives
    sign(u)·(L − (L − 1)/(1 + (|u| − 1)/(3(L − 1)))³);
  - ceilings: Sym 1, 2, 3 have L = 8, 4, 2; Asym 1, 2, 3 have L = 4, 2, 1.3 for u > 0 and 8 for u < 0;
  - u is the internal signal level, at which the probe sine at oscillator gain 1 and balance gain 1
    has amplitude 0.700;
  - the fit residual is at most 6.4 × 10⁻⁵ at the output;
  - the saturator family is that of the arm64 code (S, `0x42a20`).
- **Output saturator** (`output.json`). out = 4·C(s/4), where C is linear for |x| ≤ 0.7 and
  1 − 0.3/(1.1111112|x| + 0.22222221)³ above, with the sign of x. It also sets |out| ≤ 4 × 10⁻⁹ to
  exactly 0. The model agrees within 1.1 × 10⁻⁶ on a saw driven to 3.86. The arm64 code
  (S, `0x27530`) has these constants.

### Envelopes (`env.json`, 46 cases, and `velocity.json`)

- **Times.** Attack, decay and release take T = exp(c; 0.005, 15) s; sustain time exp(c; 0.1, 1,000) s.
  Sustain level is n.
- **Segments.** Each segment runs from y₀ to y₁ over L = T·fs frames (real L, N = ⌊L⌋). Frames
  1..N step in binary32:
  - **Linear** (ExponentialSlope off): y ← y + (y₁ − y₀)/N.
  - **Exponential** (on): y ← y·(1 − k) + t·k, with k = 3.4906588/L and t = y₀ + 1.0313935(y₁ − y₀).
    The overshot target makes the curve meet y₁ at L.

  Frame N + 1 is exactly y₁. The next segment's first step is the frame after.
- **Order.** Attack 0 → 1 starts at the frame after the note-on's grid point. Then come decay
  1 → S and sustain S → 0 over the sustain time. A note-off starts the release from the current
  gain to 0 at its grid point. The voice ends when the gain reaches 0; the output then stays exactly 0.
- **Agreement.** All 46 cases agree within 2.4 × 10⁻⁷, both slopes, every segment.
- **Binary32 quirks.** The binary32 steps explain these:
  - linear sustain time 1 does not decay, because the step is below half an ulp of 1;
  - exponential sustain time 1 decays by one ulp per frame near 1, about 0.26 % per second;
  - linear sustain time 0.9 decays like exponential sustain time 1.
- **Velocity.**
  - AmpMod m scales the envelope by 2^((a/2)(v − 64)/63), with a = lin(0..8). At m = 0.5,
    velocity 127 gives ×4 and velocity 1 gives ×¼.
  - AttackMod scales the attack time by 2^(−(a/2)(v − 64)/63).
  - At the defaults (both 0) velocity has no effect.
- **Filter envelope.** Envelope.0 drives the filter modulation above; only its sustain level
  was rendered. Being the same module class (S), it is taken to follow the same segment law.
  That is inferred, not shown.
- **Not run.** Loop modes, Legato, FreeRun, and the amplifier's keyboard amplitude, keyboard pan
  and envelope pan modulation.

### LFOs (`lfo.json`)

- **Rate.** exp(c; 3, 1,000)/60 Hz, from 0.05 to 16.67 Hz. The LFO runs from phase 0 at the first
  processed frame. With LFOGateReset it restarts at each note-on.
- **Depths.**
  - Pitch: 4n² octaves (0.1 → 0.041, 0.5 → 1.0, 0.75 → 2.26).
  - Cutoff: 4n² octaves.
  - Amplitude: gain 1 + n·LFO, within 0.5 % (square LFO, three amounts).
  - Pan: position 0.5 + n·LFO/2.
- **Shapes.**
  - 0 Sine: rising from 0.
  - 1: starts at +1, falls linearly to −1 at phase PW, then rises.
  - 2: square, +1 below phase PW and −1 above.
  - 3 and 4: random-like but seed-independent. Their outputs were not described.
- **Phase.** With reset, Phase 0.25 and 0.5 shift the start by a quarter and a half cycle.
  Free-running, Phase 0.5 shifted it by a quarter cycle. The Phase law is open.
- **Not described.** PW modulation of the oscillator: duty swings of ±0.011, ±0.063 and ±0.216
  for amounts 0.25, 0.5 and 1 were measured, but the law was not identified.

### Vibrato (`vibrato.json`)

- **Rate.** lin(0.25..10) Hz: 5.125 Hz at 0.5, measured 5.1252.
- **Depth.** exp(c; 0.0002, 1/6) octave peak.
- **Delay.** exp0(0.05..5) s of no vibrato; 0.509 s at host 0.5.
- **Fade-in.** Grows to full depth over about the FadeIn time.
- VibratoError is seed-dependent. The mod-wheel path was not run.

### Voices (`voices.json`)

- **Polyphony.** Stored 0 to 9 gives 1, 2, 4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24, 28 or 32 voices. Each was tested
  with 36 staggered held notes; the newest N sound.
- **Stealing.** A stolen voice is cut within about 2 ms, whatever its release.
- **Mono priority.**
  - 0 High: the highest held key sounds.
  - 1 Low: the lowest.
  - 2 Last (default): the latest. Releasing it returns to the latest still-held key.
- **Mono key change.** When the sounding key changes, the peak dips to 0.29–0.45 of its level about
  100 frames in and recovers within about 300 (DAN-040; `glide.json` with glide on). A release that
  leaves the sounding key unchanged has no dip. The exact form was not characterized.
- **Glide.** Pitch traces were not consistent with a simple time constant. In mono legato
  transitions the pitch passed below the start note. Unresolved.
- **Unison.** Not characterized.

## Determinism and floating point (`determinism.json`, `libm.json`, `fma_scan.txt`)

- **Repeatability.** The Defaults-preset chord is bit-identical across a re-run, blocks of 64,
  256 and 2,048, flush-to-zero with denormals-are-zero, and clock 1,760,000,000. With blocks of
  333 the note-off at frame 30,000 moves by one control period, by the grid rule.
- **libm.** Every inexact libm result moved one ulp:
  - waveform rel RMS up to 4.7 × 10⁻⁴ over the first 2,048 frames after onset, and 1.8 × 10⁻³ over
    8,192;
  - 10 ms RMS envelope within 0.0075 dB;
  - f0 within 1.2 × 10⁻⁶;
  - the noise-into-LP24 case 7 × 10⁻⁶.
- **arm64.** 814 fused multiply-adds in 130 functions, against none on x86_64. Among the twelve
  functions the scan lists are 24 in the envelope parameter function (`0x2f638`) and 20 in the
  drive saturator (`0x42a20`).
  Inferred: arm64 rounding differs at the libm scale. The binary32 sustain-time quirks may
  differ on arm64 if the segment step is fused. That was not checked.
- **Metric.** These figures set the metric in SPEC-DEVICE-ANALOG. A sample-exact comparison over
  long windows is not usable.

## Corrections to other pages

- [Device DSP](device-dsp-extracted.md#analog-results): the activity flag is 1 for the first two
  blocks after creation, before any note. The inferred clock-seeded randomness of noise and
  KeyboardError is now X. The LFO random shapes are not clock-seeded.
- [Device DSP](device-dsp-extracted.md#analog-results): "C4 261.537 Hz" is not a measurement
  error. It is the exact law above (−0.585 cent at 44.1 kHz).
- SPEC-BUNDLED-DEVICE-PARAMETERS REQ-014 holds Analog's enumeration labels as U. The renders fix
  what the values do:
  - FilterType 0–9 are LP12, LP24, BP6, BP12, Notch2, Notch4, HP12, HP24, Formant6 and Formant12
    by response;
  - FilterDrive 0–6 are off and three symmetric then three asymmetric strengths;
  - OscillatorWaveShape 0–3 are Sine, Saw, Rectangle and Noise;
  - KeyboardPriority 0–2 are High, Low and Last.

  The labels themselves stay U.

## Limits

- **What X shows.** It shows what the x86_64 code computes for the stated protocol and
  substitutions. Live's call pattern is N5's static reading. The Live-side unknowns are listed in
  the spec.
- **Static readings.** The module identities and curve declarations come from arm64 decompiler
  text and were not traced on x86_64. Each law used in the spec was confirmed by renders.
- **The measurement device.** Index 99 = 0 is outside Live's protocol. It was used only to measure
  envelope gain, never in a reference render.
- **Not rendered.** MIDI controllers, pitch bend, channel pressure and MPE; LFO 2 and the chain-2
  envelopes, taken as identical by the routing equivalence; rates other than 44.1 and 48 kHz;
  sample-rate changes; reset.

## Reproduction

```sh
sh analysis/ableton-live-12.4.3/r2/t12-analog-v1/run.sh
```

This needs gcc, python3 with numpy and scipy, objdump, the app bundle and the read-only helper
corpus. `results/cases.json` lists each reference render's SHA-256 and the imports it used.
